## r/Ultralight: Europe ultralight on a budget

Thread: <https://www.reddit.com/r/Ultralight/comments/1vm9dqd/europe_ultra_light_on_a_budget_what_would_you/>

Draft:

Your plan to buy the backpack last is good. Once the shelter and sleep system
are fixed, add food/water volume and choose a pack that fits that load instead
of guessing the capacity now.

The easiest weight savings look like this:

- A 1P tent with a real vestibule can keep shoes and a backpack off wet ground;
  you do not automatically need a 2P inner for that. Choose 2P only if the extra
  living room is worth the weight to you.
- Choose the sleeping bag by its comfort rating, not its limit rating. If you
  sleep cold, put your expected overnight low above the bag's comfort rating
  with some margin.
- R-value should follow the coldest ground you expect, not just the season
  label. A lighter inflatable plus a thin CCF pad can be modular, and pad
  R-values are generally additive when stacked.
- I would not use cotton for the liner or pillowcase. It absorbs moisture and is
  heavy for the warmth it adds. A light synthetic liner, or simply clean sleep
  clothes, is usually the better weight/drying tradeoff.

Before buying, make a spreadsheet or LighterPack with exact weights, prices and
comfort/R ratings. Prioritize warmth first, then remove duplicated functions.
Getting the big three near 3 kg is realistic without choosing fragile gear, but
durability and sleep quality matter more than chasing the lightest listing.

## r/CampingGear: Mongar 2 Pro or Giling 2 Pro

Thread: <https://www.reddit.com/r/CampingGear/comments/1vit3vm/which_tent_to_buy_naturehike_mongar_2_pro_or/>

Draft:

For the use you described, I would decide from ventilation and exposure rather
than the 15 C night temperature alone. Most of your warmth will come from the
pad and sleeping bags; a tent with less mesh can reduce drafts, but it can also
hold more condensation.

The Mongar Pro makes more sense if most trips are normal three-season camps and
you value the room created by the cross pole, two-door airflow and easier summer
ventilation. The Giling Pro is the more logical direction if exposed sites,
strong wind and colder shoulder-season use are common, but the four-season
label is not a guarantee of storm performance.

Before ordering, put the exact inner-floor dimensions next to the widths of
your two sleeping pads. For one 180 cm adult plus a six-year-old, usable width
and wall angle will tell you more than the nominal “2 person” label. Also compare
the same weight definition on both pages: minimum/trail weight and full packed
weight are often mixed in retailer listings.

I have only compared the manufacturers' specifications, not slept in these two
models, so I would give more weight to owners reporting condensation and windy
pitches in Finnish conditions.
